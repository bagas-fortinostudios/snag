Set up Snag

When it breaks

Find the message you saw. Each one has one fix.

Do this first, whatever the message says.

Sites change how they serve video every few weeks, and yt-dlp ships fixes within days. An old copy is the most common reason a download fails. Upgrade it, then try again.

Paste into Terminal
pipx upgrade yt-dlp
HTTP Error 403: Forbidden

What it means. The site refused to answer, because it could not tell the request came from a real browser.

The fix. The Chrome support is missing. Check by running yt-dlp --list-impersonate-targets. If that prints an empty table or an error, run this and try again.

Paste into Terminal
pipx install --force "yt-dlp[default,curl-cffi]"
Login required or Please wait a few minutes

What it means. Instagram will not show the video to someone who is not signed in.

The fix. Sign into Instagram in Chrome. Then quit Chrome completely with ⌘ Q, not just the window, because macOS will not let Snag read your login while Chrome is open. Run your command again. For Instagram and TikTok links, Snag turns Use my Chrome login on by itself, so the command already asks for it.

ERROR: Unable to extract

What it means. The site changed how its pages are built, and the yt-dlp on your Mac has not caught up.

The fix. Upgrade yt-dlp with the command at the top of this page. That fixes this almost every time.

command not found: snag

What it means. Snag is installed, but Terminal does not know where to look for it.

The fix. Paste this, then quit Terminal with ⌘ Q and open it again. The restart matters: the change does not take effect until then.

Paste into Terminal
echo 'export PATH="$HOME/bin:$PATH"' >> ~/.zshrc
It downloaded a whole playlist instead of one video

What it means. Your YouTube link had &list= in it, and Whole playlist was switched on under Options.

The fix. Switch Whole playlist off and get the command again. With it off, a link that carries a playlist gets only the video you were watching.

The file plays fine, but my editing software struggles with it

What it means. You have an AV1 or VP9 file. Browsers play those easily. Premiere and Resolve decode them in software, so scrubbing becomes unusable.

The fix. Use Ready to edit. It downloads H.264, which every editor handles natively, with no converting. It tops out at 1080p on YouTube. For 4K that edits, use 4K (slow), which re-encodes the best stream and takes real minutes.

The monthly check

Once a month, check the yt-dlp on your Mac before a download fails, not after.

Paste into Terminal
snag dash

A small panel opens in your browser. It shows how old your yt-dlp is, upgrades it with one button, and runs a self-test that downloads a 19-second video, checks it came out as H.264, and deletes it. It also lists what you downloaded recently.

That panel can run things because it is a small server on your own Mac, not a page on the internet. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, and closing the Terminal window stops it.

What the colour means

  • Green, under 30 days old. Fine.
  • Blue, 30 to 60 days. Upgrade it.
  • Red, over 60 days. This is probably your problem.

Rather not open the panel? pipx upgrade yt-dlp does the same upgrade on its own.

Snag is a wrapper around yt-dlp and ffmpeg, who do the actual work.

This website does not download anything. Nothing you paste here is uploaded, logged or counted.
